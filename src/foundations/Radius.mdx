# Radius

Four steps, all derived from a single `--radius` base of `0.625rem` (10px)
declared in `src/tokens.css`:

| Token | Formula | At the default base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rounded-sm` | `calc(var(--radius) - 4px)` | 6px |
| `rounded-md` | `calc(var(--radius) - 2px)` | 8px |
| `rounded-lg` | `var(--radius)` | 10px |
| `rounded-xl` | `calc(var(--radius) + 4px)` | 14px |

<Canvas of={RadiusStories.Scale} />

The four steps are close together on purpose — 6 to 14px is a narrow
band. Radius is a weak signal: a viewer reads the difference between a
card and a button from size, fill and position long before corner
rounding, so a wide radius range buys inconsistency without buying
hierarchy.

## What the kit uses

`rounded-md` (8px), on both components, and nothing else. Buttons and
inputs sit next to each other constantly, so they share a corner; the
other three steps are there for surfaces that do not exist yet — cards at
`lg`, modals at `xl`.

## The steps are offsets, not ratios

`sm` is `base − 4px`, not `base × 0.6`. That matters as soon as you change
the base:

- at `--radius: 1rem` (16px) the steps are 12 / 14 / 16 / 20px — still a
  4px spread, so a larger base makes the steps *proportionally* closer
  together and the scale flattens.
- at `--radius: 0.25rem` (4px) the steps are **0** / 2 / 4 / 8px — `sm`
  collapses to a square corner, and any component using it silently loses
  its rounding.

shadcn's own theme uses ratios (`calc(var(--radius) * 0.6)`) for this
reason. The offsets here are kept because they are what the shadcn
registry's `theme` item writes into a consuming project, and having the
same formula in both places matters more than having the better one — but
if the base ever moves far from 10px, switch both to ratios rather than
re-tuning the offsets.

## Customization

Change `--radius` in `src/tokens.css`; the four derived steps recompute
through `calc()` in the `@theme inline` block with no further edits.

Nothing in the kit hard-codes a corner value, so the base is the only
knob — with the caveat above about what happens at the extremes of its
range.
