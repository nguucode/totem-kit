# Shadows

Four elevation steps. Rather than adding a new utility namespace, these
read as `var(--shadow-sm)` through `var(--shadow-xl)`, and each resolves
per mode rather than being a fixed value.

| Token | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--shadow-sm` | 1px drop, 5% black | 1px drop, 30% black |
| `--shadow-md` | 4px + 2px, 10% black | 4px + 2px, 40% black |
| `--shadow-lg` | 10px + 4px, 10% black | 10px + 4px, 40% black |
| `--shadow-xl` | 20px + 8px, 10% black | 20px + 8px, 50% black |

## Why dark mode needs different values

A drop shadow is black at low opacity. On a white page that darkens the
pixels under it and reads as depth; on a near-black page there is almost
nothing left to darken, so the same shadow is invisible. The `.dark` block
in `tokens.css` therefore raises the alpha of every step — roughly 3× to
5× — which is why this page looks broadly the same in both themes rather
than fading out in one. Flip the **Theme** control and the shadows stay
readable.

Raising alpha only goes so far. Past a point a dark-mode shadow reads as a
dark smudge instead of elevation, which is why the system also lifts
surfaces by lightness: `--card` and `--popover` are a step *lighter* than
`--background` in dark mode. **In dark mode, surface lightness is the
primary elevation signal and the shadow is secondary.** In light mode it
is the other way around.

## Nothing uses these yet

No component in the kit sets a shadow utility — Button and TextInput are
both flat, bordered controls. These steps exist for the surfaces that will
need them (popover, dropdown, modal, toast), and are documented now so
those components adopt a shared scale instead of each inventing a
`box-shadow`.

That also means the steps are **untested against real components.** Treat
them as a starting point to tune when the first floating surface lands,
not as settled values.

## A note on rings and shadows

`box-shadow` is a single property, so a shadow and a focus ring drawn the
same way would overwrite each other. The kit draws focus with `outline`
instead — it has its own property and its own `outline-offset`, and
current browsers follow `border-radius` with it. An elevated surface can
therefore carry a shadow and a focus ring at once without either fighting
the other.

## Customization

The raw values are `--shadow-elevation-sm` through `-xl` in `:root` and
`.dark`; the `*` block maps them onto `--shadow-*` and does not need to
change.

Keep the two modes as separate decisions — copying the light values into
`.dark` is exactly the mistake the alpha bump exists to prevent.
